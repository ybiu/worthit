# WorthIt scoring rubric

Score only dimensions that fit the work unit. Each score ranges from `0` to `5`; a score is directional evidence, not a claim of exact economic value.

| Dimension | 0–1 | 2 | 3 | 4–5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `delivery_value` | No usable output or major rework required | Partial output | Usable output with follow-up | Strong or complete deliverable, directly useful |
| `practical_value` | No visible use or benefit | Local or speculative benefit | Clear benefit to a user, team, or experiment | Measurable, reusable, operational, or commercial impact |
| `quality_or_rigor` | Unreliable, incorrect, or unvalidated | Mixed quality | Acceptable for iteration | Strong validation, polish, or rigor |
| `novelty_or_insight` | Routine or derivative | Minor adaptation | Useful framing or synthesis | Distinctive insight or valuable original contribution |
| `adoption_confidence` | Very unlikely to be used | Plausible but uncertain | Likely limited use | Already adopted, deployed, merged, accepted, or strongly committed |

## Scenario defaults

- **Coding / prototype / operations:** usually use `delivery_value`, `practical_value`, `quality_or_rigor`, and `adoption_confidence`. Use `novelty_or_insight` only when relevant.
- **Research / writing:** usually use all five dimensions, treating adoption as likelihood of acceptance, follow-through, readership, or use.
- **Early experiments:** prefer `not_yet` or `promising` over a final judgement when outcome evidence does not exist.

## Confidence

- `high`: direct artifacts, measurements, test results, deployment evidence, or stakeholder confirmation support the assessment.
- `medium`: enough reported context exists for a directional assessment, with some estimates.
- `low`: major inputs or outcome evidence are missing.
